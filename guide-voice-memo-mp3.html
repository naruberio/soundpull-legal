How to convert a voice memo to MP3 on iPhone

Short answer: Voice Memos saves recordings as M4A. Save the memo to the Files app, then open it in SoundPull with MP3 selected. The conversion runs on your iPhone, so the recording is never uploaded.

Steps with SoundPull

  1. In Voice Memos, open the recording's options (the ••• button), tap Share, then Save to Files. Pick a folder you will find again.
  2. Open SoundPull. Under Output format, pick MP3.
  3. Tap From Files and choose the memo you just saved.
  4. When you see Audio extracted, tap Save / Share to save the MP3 to Files or send it to another app.

Only need part of the recording? Turn on Trim a section before you choose the file. For a quiet recording, Match the volume brings it up to a comfortable level. To join several memos into one file, see how to merge audio files.

Is there a way without an app?

Not a real one. iPhone has no built-in option to save a voice memo as MP3. What people often try instead:

  • Renaming the file from .m4a to .mp3 in Files changes only the name. The audio inside is still AAC, and players or websites that check the file will refuse it.
  • Online converters do convert, but you upload the recording to someone else's server first. Think twice before doing that with meetings, interviews or personal notes.

Before converting at all, check whether the place you are sending it to really needs MP3. Many apps and sites accept M4A as it is.

Questions

Does SoundPull send my recording to a server?
No. All processing is on-device. Your videos and audio never leave your iPhone.
Will the MP3 sound worse?
MP3 is a compressed format, and so is the M4A from Voice Memos, so converting compresses the audio once more. If you plan to edit the recording and want to avoid that, choose WAV instead; the file will be much larger.
Is SoundPull a subscription?
No. It is a one-time purchase with no subscription and no ads. Every feature is included. The App Store shows the price for your country.
What do I need?
An iPhone with iOS 16.4 or later.
